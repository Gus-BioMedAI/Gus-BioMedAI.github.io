Structuring Spanish oncology notes at Hospital Clínico San Carlos

AI Research Intern, Hospital Clínico San Carlos · February to July 2026

Lung-cancer notes are free text, so stage, ECOG or PD-L1 can't be counted without reading every note. I built a pipeline that extracts ten variables from them: histology, stage, T, N and M, ECOG, biomarkers, PD-L1, treatment and dates.

There was no labelled data, so the first step was the annotation guideline, which I defined with an oncologist at the hospital, with a boundary rule for each label. I then built the gold standard: regex and gazetteer pre-annotation, then manual annotation and review. That gave 1,432 spans across 108 notes from 43 patients, checked with a blind reliability pass. Everything ran on-premise and offline under GDPR and LOPDGDD.

Validated per patient, so no patient appears in both training and test: micro-F1 0.71 when a prediction must overlap the right span, 0.47 when boundaries must match exactly. Frozen bsc-bio-ehr-es encoder with a linear head (PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers).

InflammEye: joint-by-joint inflammation from a thermal image of the hand

University project with Hospital Universitario Ramón y Cajal · five-person team

Rheumatoid-arthritis activity is scored joint by joint. InflammEye takes a FLIR hand thermogram, locates each joint (MediaPipe), and returns a per-joint inflammation probability with a SHAP explanation (LightGBM), plus a whole-hand ThermoJIS score.

Trained on thermograms collected at the hospital: 136 patients, 3,982 joints. ThermoJIS tracks the scores rheumatologists already use: ρ = 0.81 with CDAI and 0.83 with SDAI.

I wrote most of the code, from the model to the app: a FastAPI model service, a React and TypeScript front end, and Supabase auth and storage. Diego Benito worked on the temperature features and debugging, helped with the data and was our main contact with the hospital's clinicians. The rest of the team wrote the market and business plan. A research prototype, not a diagnostic device. Watch the demo (it uses dummy images and data).

Multimodal survival model for lung cancer, tested with oncologists

Master's thesis, UPM · placement at Hospital Clínico San Carlos · supervised by Laura Llorente-Sanz · defended July 2026

Does adding CT radiomics to clinical variables improve survival prediction in non-small-cell lung cancer? I built a late-fusion model on the public NSCLC-Radiomics cohort (421 patients). A shared variable schema links it to the NER pipeline above, so the same variables can come from hospital notes or the public cohort.

The combined model ranked patients better than either source alone (C-index 0.587, against 0.577 imaging-only and 0.539 clinical-only). The gain is small, and I report it with calibration (integrated Brier score 0.21).

I then took it to the intended users. I presented the model to oncologists in three sessions (two on site, one online) and ran a Technology Acceptance Model survey (5 oncologists, 3 biomedical engineers). The oncologists rated usefulness and adoption intent 4.4 to 4.8 out of 5.

How I work.

Most of my work sits between a hospital and a model.

Clinical AI often performs well on a test set and then fails in a real clinic, so I spend most of my time on the data: what each field means, who entered it, and what a clinician needs to see before trusting an output. That is why the NER project started with a guideline written with an oncologist, and the thesis ended in front of oncologists, not just a test set.

My bachelor's thesis at Politecnico di Milano simulated radiomic data to validate imaging methods. Outside work: film, reading, piano and photography.
